## 0. Executive Summary (TL;DR)
### The Problem
Technology defines social rules “as implementation,” but the perspectives (values, goals, and optimizations) driving that implementation are often invisible.

### The Goal
We need more than just “regulation for punishment.” We need public transparency infrastructure that allows society to understand, verify, and improve systems.

### The Solution
IDTO is neither a regulator nor a rating agency. It proposes a protocol (common language) to make transparency “recordable and reviewable” worldwide.

---

## 0.1 Fixed vs. Open Elements
### Fixed (Constitution)
- **Purpose:** Shift digital decision-making from “impression” to “fact and verification.”
- **Positioning:** IDTO is a provider of a common language (protocol), not a judge.
- **The 3 Pillars (Ch. 3) & 6 Fixed Keywords (Ch. 4):** These are non-negotiable core principles.

### Open (RFC: Request for Comments)
Operational details, granularity of data, incentives, and verification methods are gathered in **Chapter 8 (Future Challenges)** for community discussion.

---

## 1. Introduction: Why IDTO?
Our society now relies on digital systems for everything—search, social media, payments, healthcare, and education. While convenient, technology is effectively “writing the law” through implementation. We often cannot see the “intent” behind these systems. IDTO is a proposal for a **Public Infrastructure for Transparency** to bridge this gap.

---

## 2. Ethical Foundation: “Justice” as Error-Correction
The core of justice in this draft is **the ability to correct errors**. No system is perfect. Reliability comes from the ability to discover, verify, and remediate mistakes. IDTO aims to provide practical tools to return digital decision-making to a foundation of facts rather than perceptions.

---

## 3. The Three Pillars (Conceptual Protocol)
### 3.1 Error-Correction as an OS
We do not assume perfection. Instead, we value systems that can be patched. The focus is not on “who is right,” but on “how we fix it.”

### 3.2 Recording “Intent & Change”
Every participant must connect at least two points:
- **Intent:** What is the system trying to achieve? (Optimization goals)
- **Change:** What was modified? (Change Logs)

This provides an objective evidence base to verify whether implementation matches intent.

### 3.3 Protocol, Not a Platform
IDTO is not a “walled garden.” It is a common language (protocol). We provide the “ground of facts” so that third parties can build their own analysis and visualization tools.

---

## 4. The 6 Fixed Keywords (Constitutional Level)
To avoid international misunderstanding, these six principles are fixed:
- **Good-faith by default:** Designed for improvement, not punishment.
- **Non-regulatory:** No enforcement or sanctions. We provide the protocol.
- **Traceable & auditable:** Centered on Change Logs to enable learning (not punitive audits).
- **No rating:** No scoring or rankings. We stick to facts and procedures.
- **Federated:** No centralized data silos. Original data stays with the owner.
- **Politically neutral:** Providing the foundation for facts, not ideologies.

---

## 5. The “Three-Layer” Structure
### 5.1 IDTOpedia (Open Knowledge Base)
A collaborative encyclopedia explaining digital accountability based on evidence.

### 5.2 Open Disclosure Registry (Structured Ledger)
Machine-readable primary information (Intent, Data Handling, Change Logs).

### 5.3 Verification Layer (Public Peer Review)
A layer where experts and the community review disclosures to ensure they are consistent and sufficient.

---

## 6. The Metaphor: “A README for Society”
Think of the digital world as a massive, complex source code. IDTO is the standard format for the README file. We do not write the content for everyone; we define the headers so that everyone else can read, verify, and update the “social code” in a way that everyone understands.

---

## 8. Future Challenges (RFC / Invitation to Discuss)
We intentionally leave the following points open for global debate:
- **8.1 Independence:** How do we protect IDTO from capture by states or corporations?
- **8.2 Change Governance:** Who decides when the protocol itself needs to change?
- **8.3 Traceability Specs:** How do we standardize “Change Logs” across different industries?
- **8.4 Federated Operations:** How do we ensure interoperability without centralizing power?
- **8.5 Funding & Neutrality:** How do we maintain financial independence?
- **8.6 Privacy & Safety:** How do we ensure transparency doesn’t expose sensitive personal data or security flaws?
