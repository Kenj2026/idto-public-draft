# Contributing

Thank you for your interest in the IDTO public draft.

This repository hosts a **draft (not established)** concept for a **non-regulatory, no-rating, federated transparency protocol**.  
We welcome discussion and improvements while keeping the core concept consistent.

---

## Where to post

### 1) RFC (design discussions)
Use **Discussions → RFC** for open design questions (especially topics referenced in **Chapter 8: Future Challenges (RFC)**).

Good for:
- Governance and neutrality
- Change logs / traceability specifications
- Federation and interoperability approaches
- Incentive and funding models (without rating)
- Privacy & safety guardrails (including staged disclosure)

### 2) Q&A (questions)
Use **Discussions → Q&A** for:
- Clarifications about the document
- Terminology and definitions
- “How should I contribute?” questions

### 3) Announcements (updates)
**Discussions → Announcements** is used for:
- Publishing new versions (e.g., V5.0)
- Major repository updates or housekeeping notes

### 4) Issues (actionable edits)
Use **Issues** for concrete, actionable items such as:
- Wording that is easily misunderstood
- Missing references or links
- Specific edits you propose (include suggested replacement text)

Useful links:
- Discussions: https://github.com/Kenj2026/idto-public-draft/discussions
- Issues: https://github.com/Kenj2026/idto-public-draft/issues

## What is fixed vs. open

### Fixed (Constitution)
The following are **non-negotiable** core principles of the IDTO concept:
- **3 Pillars:** Error-Correction as an OS / Recording Intent & Change / Protocol, not a Platform
- **6 Keywords:** Good-faith by default / Non-regulatory / Traceable & auditable / No rating / Federated / Politically neutral

Proposals that contradict these are out of scope for this draft.

Examples of out-of-scope directions (non-exhaustive):
- Turning IDTO into an approval, enforcement, compliance, or certification body
- Introducing scoring, rankings, labels, or “official grades”
- Centralizing records into a single mandatory silo
- Advocating partisan or political objectives as protocol goals

### Open (RFC)
Operational details and implementation choices are intentionally open for discussion, including:
- Disclosure granularity (minimal core vs extensions)
- Incentive design and adoption pathways (without rating)
- Verification models and improvement loops (patch-oriented review)
- Federation mechanisms and interoperability rules
- Safety and privacy constraints (including staged disclosure)

## How to write a good proposal

When you propose changes (in Discussions, Issues, or PRs), please include:

### Proposal template (recommended)
- **Proposed change:** What you want to change (quote the relevant section if possible)
- **Rationale:** Why this improves clarity, consistency, feasibility, or adoption
- **Trade-offs / risks:** What could go wrong (cost, complexity, capture risk, privacy risk, etc.)
- **Alternatives (optional):** Other approaches you considered
- **Success criteria (optional):** What would “better” look like in practice?

For RFC topics, it helps to propose a patch-style output:
- claim → evidence → impact → proposed patch → alternatives → COI disclosure

## Pull requests (PRs)

PRs are welcome for:
- Improving clarity without changing the core concept
- Adding a glossary or definitions
- Adding templates (e.g., disclosure minimal core)
- Adding examples / case studies (clearly marked as examples)

For PRs that affect **Constitution-level** sections, please start with an RFC discussion first and link the RFC in the PR.

---

## Decision notes (how outcomes are recorded)

When an RFC converges or a PR is merged:
- Summarize the decision and rationale in the relevant Discussion thread, and
- Reflect the outcome in the draft text and/or `CHANGELOG.md` where appropriate.

---

## Conduct

Please keep discussion constructive and evidence-oriented.
- Assume good faith; focus on improving the draft rather than scoring participants.
- Avoid ratings, rankings, or labels of people, organizations, or services.
- When criticizing a claim, prefer **claim + evidence + suggested patch** over general assertions.
